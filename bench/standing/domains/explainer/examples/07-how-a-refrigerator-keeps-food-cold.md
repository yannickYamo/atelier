---
request: Explain how a refrigerator keeps food cold to someone with no background in it.
---
A refrigerator never makes cold. It removes heat from inside and dumps it into your kitchen. That one idea explains everything else.

Inside the appliance runs a sealed loop of tubing holding a fluid called refrigerant. This fluid boils at a very low temperature. In the panel inside the fridge, it soaks up heat from the air and food, turning into gas. The warmth that was in your milk is now riding away in that gas.

A pump called the compressor then squeezes the gas hard. Squeezing makes it hot, hotter than your kitchen air. The gas flows to coils at the back or underneath and sheds that heat into the room. Cooling down, it becomes liquid again and returns to start over.

Imagine wiping a damp cloth across your arm. As the water evaporates, your skin feels cool, because evaporation carries heat away with it. The refrigerant repeats that trick endlessly inside a closed circle.

A thermostat watches the temperature for you. When the inside air drifts too warm, the compressor switches on. When things are cold enough, it rests. That is why you hear the hum come and go through the day.

In short, your fridge is a heat pump, quietly moving warmth from your food into your kitchen.
