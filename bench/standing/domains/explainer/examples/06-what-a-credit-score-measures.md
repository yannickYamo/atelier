---
request: Explain what a credit score measures to someone with no background in it.
---
Lenders face a simple problem. They hand over money today and hope you pay it back later. They cannot read your mind, so they look at your past behaviour instead. A credit score is the number that summarises that behaviour.

It is built from records of how you have handled borrowed money. Did you pay your card bill on time? Do you owe a lot compared with your limits? How long have your accounts been open? Have you applied for lots of credit recently? All of this is squeezed into one figure.

Picture the reputation you build at a corner shop over several years. If you always settle your tab, the owner trusts you with more. Miss a few payments, and that goodwill quietly shrinks. A credit score does the same job, but in numbers, for strangers.

A higher number tells lenders you look like a smaller risk. That usually means easier approvals and lower interest rates. A lower number does not mean you are bad with money. Often it just means you have little borrowing history so far.

Your score is not fixed either. Consistent payments and modest balances tend to lift it over time.

In short, a credit score measures how reliably you have repaid money you borrowed.
