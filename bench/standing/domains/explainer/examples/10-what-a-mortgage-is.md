---
request: Explain what a mortgage is to someone with no background in it.
---
Most people cannot pay for a house in cash. A mortgage is the loan that solves that problem. A bank or other lender gives you the money to buy a property. You then repay it gradually, usually over twenty-five or thirty years.

Two things separate a mortgage from an ordinary loan. First, you pay interest, which is the lender's fee for the service. Second, the property itself acts as security for the debt. If you stop paying, the lender can take the house and sell it.

Picture a friend lending you money for a bicycle on one condition. Until your final payment arrives, the bicycle remains theirs to reclaim. That safety net is why mortgage interest rates are lower than credit card rates.

You normally begin with a deposit, a portion of the price paid upfront. The bigger your deposit, the less you borrow, and the cheaper your loan becomes. Each monthly payment then does two jobs at once. Part of it covers interest, and part reduces the amount you still owe. In the early years, most of your payment is interest. Later, more of it eats into the balance itself.

In short, a mortgage lets you live in a home now while paying for it slowly, with the home itself as the lender's guarantee.
