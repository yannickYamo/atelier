---
request: Explain what cholesterol does in the body to someone with no background in it.
---
Cholesterol has a bad reputation, but your body cannot function without it. It is a waxy, fat-like substance found in every one of your cells.

Your liver makes most of what you need. The rest comes from food, mainly animal products such as eggs, meat and dairy.

Inside each cell, cholesterol sits in the outer membrane and keeps it firm yet flexible. Without it, your cells would be too floppy to hold their shape. Cholesterol is also a raw material. Your body turns it into vitamin D, bile acids that digest fat, and hormones like oestrogen, testosterone and cortisol.

Because cholesterol does not dissolve in blood, it travels wrapped in protein packages called lipoproteins. Imagine delivery vans moving cargo along a road network. The LDL vans drop cholesterol off at tissues that need it. The HDL vans collect leftovers and carry them back to the liver.

Trouble starts when too many LDL vans crowd the route. Cholesterol can then seep into artery walls and build up as stiff plaque. That narrows your blood vessels and raises your risk of heart attack and stroke.

In short, cholesterol is essential building material. The danger lies in how much circulates, not in the substance itself.
