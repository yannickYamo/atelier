# Drafting a short term summary from a signed-off term sheet

1. Head the document with the title "Term Summary:" followed by the deal name in the form the term sheet uses.
2. Under the title, before section 1, add a one-line italic status marker, and the status is one of: Draft, For Review, Agreed.
3. Open with a Parties section that names both parties exactly as the term sheet does: full legal name, entity type and registered number.
4. Give each party a short name in bold and quote marks on first mention, then use only that short name afterwards.
5. Never write "the Company" for either side in a mutual document; it invites a fight about who is meant.
6. Sections run in this order: Parties, Purpose, Key Terms, Obligations, Exclusions, Term and Termination, Governing Law, Open Points, Sign-Off.
7. Number the sections 1 to 9 in their headings, so people can say "see 4" on a call.
8. Purpose is one sentence of no more than 40 words; it says why information is changing hands and nothing else.
9. Key Terms is a table with the columns Term, Position and Term Sheet Ref.
10. The Term Sheet Ref column cites the term sheet clause for every row in the form "TS cl. 4.2"; no row goes without one.
11. Carry over every figure from the term sheet unchanged: dates, periods, caps, notice days. Don't turn 24 months into 2 years.
12. Write money as currency code then figure, "GBP 250,000", and write periods as a numeral with its unit, "30 days".
13. Between 6 and 9 rows in Key Terms; if I need more, this isn't a short-form deal and I should tell the partner so.
14. Obligations is a numbered list of no more than 6 items, each one sentence, starting "Each party shall" or "Neither party shall" where the duty is mutual.
15. Use "shall" for obligations and "may" for permissions; never "will", "must" or "should" in the Obligations section.
16. Check an obligation is truly mutual before writing "each party"; where the term sheet makes it one-way, name the party who bears it.
17. Exclusions sets out the carve-outs as lettered sub-items, (a), (b) and so on, four at most, and only the ones the term sheet actually agreed.
18. Define each capitalised term once, in bold and quote marks where it first appears, and don't capitalise anything I haven't defined.
19. Don't use "reasonable endeavours", "best endeavours" or "commercially reasonable efforts" unless the term sheet uses those exact words; then quote it and cite the clause.
20. No "and/or", no "including but not limited to", no "hereinafter", no "witnesseth".
21. Term and Termination gives the start date, the duration, the survival period and the notice period, in that order, in at most 3 sentences.
22. Think about the day after termination: who holds what, and is there a duty to return or destroy it within a stated number of days?
23. Governing Law is one sentence naming the law and the courts, and it says whether jurisdiction is exclusive or non-exclusive.
24. Open Points is a table with the columns Point, Term Sheet Says and Recommended Position.
25. Put in Open Points anything the term sheet is silent or ambiguous on; never fill a gap quietly with my own preferred wording.
26. Order the Open Points by how much money or exposure rides on each, biggest first.
27. Keep Open Points to 5 rows at most, and where there are none write "None identified" under the heading rather than dropping the section.
28. In the Term Sheet Says column quote the term sheet's wording in quote marks with its clause, or write "Silent".
29. Sign-Off is three lines: "Drafted by:", "Reviewed by:" and "Term sheet relied on:".
30. Give the term sheet's version and date in Sign-Off exactly as printed on it, e.g. "v3, 14 May 2025".
31. The whole summary fits on one page: 350 to 500 words, counting the tables.
32. Sentences stay under 35 words; a longer sentence in a contract summary is hiding a second obligation.
33. Don't summarise a term more favourably to our client than the term sheet reads; the summary goes to both sides.
34. Flag in Open Points any term that looks unenforceable or out of line for the sector, even if both sides have agreed it.
35. Write dates in full as "1 March 2026", never 01/03/26.
36. Write "party" and "parties" in lower case.
37. No adjectives of comfort: drop "robust", "comprehensive", "standard", "customary" and "usual".
38. Where a number appears in both words and figures in the term sheet and they disagree, use neither and raise it in Open Points.
39. Read each obligation as the other side's lawyer would, looking for the reading that hurts.
40. End the document with the sentence "This summary does not create legal relations." on its own line after Sign-Off.
