# Your writing rules — one page, thirty minutes

*This exact template is versioned with the study so the record shows precisely what prompted the
page you write. It deliberately asks open questions rather than teaching any structure: the page
must be yours, not an echo of ours.*

---

Hi — before anything else in this study, we need one thing from you, and it only works if it comes
first: **your own description of what makes your writing yours.**

## The rules of the exercise

- **Thirty minutes, timed.** When the timer ends, stop. Rough is fine; rough is the point.
- **One page** — roughly 400–600 words. Bullets, prose, fragments: whatever you'd actually write.
- **Your words only. No AI assistance of any kind** — no drafting, no polishing, no "just checking
  phrasing". You'll be asked to confirm this.
- You may glance at your own past pieces if that's how you'd naturally do it — just tell us whether
  you did.
- **Don't revise it later.** Date it, save it, send it once. A second draft is a different
  document and we can't use it.
- Write it **before** you see anything else from this study — no candidate rules, no generated
  text, no other materials. That ordering is the whole reason we're asking now.

## What to write

Imagine a capable writer has to produce a piece in your place tomorrow, in your usual work type,
and this page is everything they get. Tell them what matters. Some questions people find useful —
use any, ignore any:

- What do you always do that most writers in your field don't?
- What do you refuse to do, even when it would be easier or expected?
- How does a piece of yours typically open, move, and end — and why that way?
- What does "too much" look like in your writing? What does "not enough" look like?
- Are there things you do only sometimes? When — and when deliberately not?
- What would make you cringe if you saw it published under your name?
- What's the difference between a piece that sounds like you and one that merely covers the topic?

There are no wrong answers and no required format. A page that says "I don't really know, but here
are five things I notice" is a perfectly good page.

## What happens to it

It goes in a sealed envelope, so to speak: **nobody uses it, reads it into any tool, or judges it
in this study.** It exists because it can only be collected uncontaminated once, and a later study
will ask a question only this page can answer. You'll see it again then.

---

**When done:** save as a plain text or markdown file, add the date and one line at the top —
*"Written in 30 minutes, no AI assistance, [did / did not] look at my own pieces"* — and send it
back before you send anything else.
